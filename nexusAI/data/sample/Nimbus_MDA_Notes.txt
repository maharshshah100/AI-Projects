Nimbus Retail Inc. — Management Discussion & Analysis (MD&A)
Fiscal Year 2025 Annual Report — Notes to the Financial Statements

OVERVIEW OF FISCAL YEAR 2025 PERFORMANCE

Fiscal year 2025 was a year of continued top-line growth for Nimbus Retail,
with revenue reaching $150.0 million, up 8.7% from $138.0 million in FY2024.
However, this growth came at a cost to profitability. Net income declined
to $7.35 million from $10.275 million in the prior year, a decrease of
28.5%. Management believes this divergence between revenue growth and
earnings reflects a combination of input cost inflation, competitive
pricing pressure, and elevated logistics expenses that are discussed in
detail below.

GROSS MARGIN AND COST OF GOODS SOLD

Gross margin declined to 36.0% in FY2025 from 39.1% in FY2024 and 40.0%
in FY2023. The primary driver was a sustained increase in supplier input
costs across our top three product categories, which our procurement team
was unable to fully offset through vendor renegotiation during the fiscal
year. Additionally, the company made a strategic decision in Q2 FY2025 to
hold retail pricing steady in several categories despite rising input
costs, in order to defend market share against a new regional competitor.
Management is actively renegotiating supplier contracts ahead of FY2026
and expects gross margin to partially recover, though not to FY2023
levels, given the structurally higher cost environment.

OPERATING EXPENSES

Total operating expenses grew to $41.0 million in FY2025 from $38.0
million in FY2024. Selling, general and administrative expenses rose to
support the opening of four new retail locations during the year.
Marketing spend also increased as the company invested in a brand
refresh campaign intended to support the pricing strategy described
above. Research and development spend remains modest relative to peers,
reflecting the company's current focus on retail operations rather than
proprietary technology development.

LIQUIDITY AND CASH POSITION

Cash and cash equivalents declined to $8.0 million at fiscal year-end
FY2025, down from $12.0 million in FY2024 and $15.0 million in FY2023.
This decline reflects the combined effect of lower operating cash
generation, continued capital investment in new store locations, and the
company's dividend policy discussed below. The current ratio declined to
1.90x and the quick ratio to 1.04x, both still above the 1.0x threshold
generally considered adequate for short-term obligations, though the
downward trend across all three years is one the Board is monitoring
closely.

CAPITAL STRUCTURE AND DEBT

Total debt (short-term plus long-term) increased to $44.0 million in
FY2025 from $34.0 million in FY2024, primarily to fund new store capital
expenditures and to partially offset the decline in operating cash flow.
The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 0.96x from 0.67x over the same period.
Interest expense increased correspondingly to $3.2 million. Management
considers the current leverage level to be within an acceptable range
given the company's asset base, but has committed to moderating the pace
of new debt issuance in FY2026 pending improvement in operating cash
generation.

WORKING CAPITAL

Accounts receivable grew 22.7% in FY2025, outpacing revenue growth of
8.7%, reflecting extended payment terms offered to several large
wholesale customers as part of the competitive response strategy
described above. Inventory grew 29.2% over the same period, reflecting
both the new store openings and a deliberate decision to increase
safety stock levels in response to supply chain volatility experienced
earlier in the fiscal year. Management is targeting a reduction in both
receivables days and inventory days during FY2026 as new stores mature
and supply chain conditions normalize.

DIVIDEND POLICY

The Board declared dividends of $11.85 million in FY2025, compared to
net income of $7.35 million for the same period. This marks the second
consecutive year in which dividends have exceeded net income, following
$8.275 million in dividends against $10.275 million in net income in
FY2024. The Board has reaffirmed its commitment to the current dividend
policy as a signal of confidence to shareholders, funding the difference
through cash reserves and, increasingly, debt financing. The Board
acknowledges this is not sustainable indefinitely absent an improvement
in underlying profitability and has placed the dividend policy on its
FY2026 strategic review agenda.

OUTLOOK AND STRATEGIC PRIORITIES FOR FY2026

Management's priorities for fiscal year 2026 are: (1) renegotiating
supplier contracts to recover gross margin, (2) moderating new debt
issuance and improving operating cash flow, (3) reducing working capital
days through inventory and receivables management, and (4) a Board-level
review of the dividend policy given two consecutive years of payout
exceeding earnings. Management does not expect a return to FY2023 margin
levels but is targeting gross margin stabilization in the 37-38% range.

RISK FACTORS

Key risks to the FY2026 outlook include continued input cost inflation,
further competitive pricing pressure, execution risk on the four new
store locations opened during FY2025 reaching maturity, and the
company's increasing reliance on debt financing amid declining cash
reserves. The company's largest supplier relationship represents
approximately 18% of total cost of goods sold; any disruption to this
relationship would have a material adverse effect on gross margin in the
near term.

GOVERNANCE AND LEADERSHIP

Nimbus Retail's Board of Directors consists of seven members, five of
whom are independent. The Audit Committee, chaired by an independent
director with a background in public accounting, meets quarterly to
review financial reporting and internal controls. There were no material
weaknesses identified in internal controls over financial reporting
during fiscal year 2025.
